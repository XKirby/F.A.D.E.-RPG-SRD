F.A.D.E. RPG System Reference Document

Character Creation

Character Creation is the process that both players and gamemasters follow before placing characters of importance within their world. There are several properties and values to calculate and determine during this process to fully realize any particular character. Some of these properties are mentioned in both the Core Rules and Introduction pages and will be further explained and fleshed out here.

Base Stats

Each character has Base Stats of 5; In other words, Physique, Ego, and Instinct all start at a value of 5. With this in mind, their Health, Stamina, and Energy start at a value of 10.

Note

To remind you of how much Health, Stamina, and Energy you may have, your maximum Health is equal to your Physique + Instinct, your maximum Stamina is equal to your Physique + Ego, and your maximum Energy is equal to your Ego + Instinct.

Major Properties

Besides the previously mentioned, each character has various Major Properties that they may start with. These properties are as followed:

Level

A character’s Level is a quick determining factor for how strong a character is. Whenever you gain one or more Levels, you become more powerful. Characters start at Level 0.

Tags

A character can have one or more Tags. These Tags include the following list:

  • Element Tag Resistance: This character Resists damage dealt and recovery gained from this Element.
  • Element Tag Weakness: This character is Weak to damage dealt and recovery gained from this Element.
  • Gear Tag Adept: This character gains a +10 bonus to d% Checks that reference Gear with this Tag.
  • Gear Tag Inept: This character gains a -10 penalty to d% Checks that reference Gear with this Tag.
  • Gear Slot Tag Count: This character can equip a number of Gear in the listed Gear Slot with that Gear Slot Tag.
  • Race Tag: This character has ancestry with this Tag. Race Tags are listed further down on this page.

Heft

A character’s Heft is a multiplier to their carrying capacity. The higher a character’s Heft, the more that character can effectively lift and carry. A character can hold Gear with total Weight less than or equal to their Physique x Heft.

Size

A character’s Size determines how large or how small that character is; A larger character has more Health and Heft at the cost of Evasion, whereas a smaller character has more Evasion as the cost of their Health and Heft. The following Sizes are available:

  • Tiny: A Tiny character has a base of 20 Evasion, 1 Heft, and one third the Health of a Medium character, rounded up. Tiny characters are less than or equal to 1 foot tall.
  • Small: A Small character has a base of 10 Evasion, 2 Heft, and one half the Health of a Medium character, rounded up. Small characters are close to about 2 to 3 feet tall.
  • Medium: A Medium character has a base of 0 Evasion, 3 Heft, and the normal formula for Health. Medium characters range from 4 to 7 feet tall.
  • Large: A Large character has a base of -10 Evasion, 4 Heft, and one and a half times as much Health as a Medium character, rounded up. Large characters range from 8 to 10 feet tall.
  • Huge: A Huge character has a base of -20 Evasion, 6 Heft, and two times as much Health as a Medium character. Huge characters range from 11 to 20 feet tall.
  • Gargantuan: A Gargantuan character has a base of -30 Evasion, 9 Heft, and has three times as much Health as a Medium character. Gargantuan characters range from 25 to 50 feet tall. Players can’t start as Gargantuan characters of Levels lower than 10.
  • Titanic: A Titanic character has a base of -40 Evasion, 15 Heft, and has five times as much Health as a Medium character. Titanic characters range from 100 to 1,000 feet tall. Players can’t start as Titanic characters of Levels lower than 20.
  • Astronomic: An Astronomic character has a base of -50 Evasion, 30 Heft, and has ten times as much Health as a Medium character. Astronomic characters range from 5,000 to 10,000 feet tall. Players can’t start as Astronomic characters.

Defense

As mentioned on other pages, Defense reduces incoming damage. Characters start with 0 Global Defense.

Aptitudes

As mentioned on other pages, Aptitudes determine how proficient and effective you are with certain Abilities. To reiterate from those sources, each Aptitude has a fixed range of -50 to +50, with positive Aptitudes counting as Boosts and negative Aptitudes counting as Penalties. You can only have a number of Aptitude Boosts and Aptitude Penalties equal to your Level x 2, and for each Aptitude Penalty you take you may apply that many more Aptitude Boosts.

Gear

Player characters start with up to two pieces of Common Gear at Level 0, but can start with more or better Gear at the GM’s discretion.

Abilities

Each character has a unique set of Abilities they have as either Innate passives or Activated with some form of cost. Abilities have various Properties. Characters start with no unique Abilities at Level 0, but may gain new Abilities either through Gear or through Ability Points earned when Leveling Up.

Experience and Leveling Up

Characters gain Experience during the game, usually from combat scenarios or at the GM’s discretion. If a character has 100 or more Experience, they Level Up and lose 100 Experience until they have below 100 Experience. As a Character increases in Level, the following occurs:

  • That character gains 2 Boosts divided as they choose among Physique, Ego, and/or Instinct per Level gained. (For example, they may put 10 points in Physique, or they may put 5 into both Physique and Ego.) Be sure to scale your Health, Stamina, and Energy accordingly.
  • That character gains 2 Boosts divided as they choose among their current and/or any new Aptitudes per Level gained. That character may also gain up to 2 Aptitude Penalties per Level gained, either new or current, to gain that many more Aptitude Boosts.
  • That character gains 1 Ability Point to spend on new or current Abilities for each Level reached. Any Ability additions or changes may be made now. (For example, if you Level Up from Level 2 to Level 3, you gain 3 Ability Points during that Level Up.)

Important

The GM chooses what Level the players start and end at. As a GM, it’s your job to make sure everyone knows and is okay with this. The recommended Level range for a larger campaign is from Levels 1 to 30.

Since the GM determines the maximum Level that both player and non-player characters can reach, be sure to talk to your GM about the scope of the session/campaign they’re hosting so you can get a good estimate on how to build your character.

Additionally, the Experience gained during the game scales down a number of points equal to your current Level, to a minimum of 1. (For example, if you gain 10 Experience at Level 0, the Experience gained that way when at Level 1 becomes 9 instead.)

Note

As characters start at Level 0 normally, if your GM has you start at Level 1 or higher, act as though you started at Level 0 and gained enough Experience to Level Up that many times.

Tip

Experience, while recommended as the source in which you Level Up, may not work for your campaign. If that is the case, you may opt for the Milestone system instead; If a player does something noteworthy, you can grant them the ability to immediately Level Up. Be wary when using this system, as you don’t want to have a huge Level disparity amongst your party’s players.
