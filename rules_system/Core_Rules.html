F.A.D.E. RPG System Reference Document

Core Rules

This page lists the Core Rules the system uses, which are based on the Core Concepts found in the Introduction page.

Base Stats

The Base Stats each character has within this system are Physique, Ego, and Instinct. As mentioned within the Introduction page, Physique represents your physical being, Ego represents your mental knowledge, and Instinct represents your willpower. Each of the Base Stats scale up and down in increments of 5. Additionally, each pair of them grant a specific resource, found below.

Health, Stamina, and Energy

Health, Stamina, and Energy are the primary resources each character has in this system. Each resource serves a unique purpose.

Health is equal to Physique + Instinct and determines the life force of that character.

  • If a character’s Health hits zero, that character is knocked unconscious and can no longer act.
  • If a character’s Health hits into the negative, that character starts dying by bleeding out slowly. Characters that bleed out lose Health equal to their Level at the start of their turn.
  • At the start of that Character’s turn after potential bleed out from dying, if you’re still unconscious or dying, you may expend 5 Energy to perform a d% Check requiring Instinct + Aptitude + d% with a Target Value of 50, plus how much Health you’re in the negative. If you succeed, set your Health to 1 and you stablize, no longer unconscious or dying.
  • Characters die whenever their Health hits negatively equal to or lower than its maximum value.

Stamina is equal to Physique + Ego and determines the physical and mental force of that character.

  • Characters can’t use actions that require more than their current Stamina.
  • Normally, Stamina can only be reduced down to a minimum of zero.

Energy is equal to Ego + Instinct and determines the spiritual force of that character.

  • Utilizing your Energy as a resource is extremely risky as it directly impacts your soul; Characters can’t recover Health unless they have at least zero Energy, and characters that hit Energy into the negative equal to or lower than their maximum snuff out their souls.

Caution

Any character that snuffs out their own soul, including player characters, relinquishes their free will. As a GM, you may decide what this means. Examples include permanent character death, taking full control of that character once they recover, demonic or otherworldly possession, etc. YOU MUST MAKE SURE YOUR PLAYERS KNOW WHAT THEY’RE GETTING THEMSELVES INTO IF THEY DO THIS.

Aptitudes

Aptitudes are used to determine how effective you are with certain Abilities beyond average capabilities. Aptitudes should be vague enough to be useful, but precise enough to not encompass everything. (For example, “Melee Aptitude” could work if your character is generally good with melee abilities, while “Gear Aptitude” is too vague and can encompass far too much of gameplay.)

An Aptitude Boost is equal to +5 in a positive Aptitude, while an Aptitude Penalty is equal to -5 in a negative Aptitude. Characters can have up to a number of Boosts equal to Level x 2 + Penalties and up to a number of Penalties equal to Level x 2. Penalties are optional; You choose to actively take them to receive Boosts in other ways.

Abilities and Action Points

Characters have a pool of Action Points that allow them to act a certain number of times within a dangerous situation. Characters are initially created having a maximum of 3 Action Points.

Abilities are the general way characters interact within the game and can consume anywhere from 0 to 3 Action Points as an upfront cost. Abilities have various Properties, such as Requirements needed to fully activate, Prerequisites to learn them, a Frequency with which they can activate in succession, a Duration to determine how long they last, and more. These will be further explained in the Creating Abilities page.

Note

Abilities use their name as a reference to determine where their Effects come from. If you apply an Ability to a target that is already referencing an Ability with its name, the old Ability’s Effects are replaced with the new Ability’s Effects, even if they’re entirely different Abilities.

Gear

Characters can use various pieces of Gear to further augment their capabilities. Gear comes in many forms, such as Consumable or Equipment.

A character may equip one Head Gear, one Body Gear, one Foot Gear, up to two Hand Gear, and up to 3 Accessory Gear at a given time, although this can change in various ways during gameplay. Each piece of Gear comes with a set of Tags that help explain how they functionally work and feel. Gear may also bestow new Abilities to characters that equip those pieces.

Gear also has Density and Durability; The more Density a piece of Gear has, the more durable and heavy it is. Density works similarly to a character’s Global Defense in that it blocks all forms of incoming damage dealt to it. Durability, on the other hand, works like a character’s Health; If a piece of Gear’s Health ever hits zero, that piece of Gear is completely destroyed.

Additionally, Gear has Heft and Weight. A piece of Gear’s Weight is also determined by its Size; Like a character of that particular Size, a piece of Gear’s Heft related to that Size, and its Weight is equal to its Density x Heft.

Defense

Characters, usually those wearing armor, have Defense as their secondary form of Damage Mitigation. Defense comes in many flavors, separated into two groups; Global Defense and Conditional Defense.

Global Defense is stronger, as it protects against every form damage can and may take within the game. However, it’s rarely found compared to Conditional Defense. Conditional Defense is more frequently found, but is weaker as it has restrictions to what it applies to. Each Conditional Defense you have can stack within their specific parameters, as well as stacking with Global Defense.

Characters are initially created having 0 Global Defense.

Evasion

Characters have Evasion as both the primary form of Effect Mitigation and Damage Mitigation. Evasion scales as though it’s an Aptitude and is strictly Global; It takes effects whenever a character is defending against any d% Check and adds directly to that character’s roll result as the roll’s Aptitude value.

Evasion is optional; Since it functions as an Aptitude, you may have other Aptitudes to choose from in place of it. Even if you don’t, you may choose not to use Evasion and instead set the Aptitude portion of the d% Check equal to -50 to make yourself more likely to hit.
