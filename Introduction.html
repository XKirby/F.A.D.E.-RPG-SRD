F.A.D.E. RPG System Reference Document

Introduction

Welcome to the System Reference Document for the Tabletop Roleplaying Game of F.A.D.E., or “Fighter’s Arena, Destructive Encounters”. This page will give a rundown on the core concepts and philosophies found within the game.

Core Concepts

The following Core Concepts of this TTRPG system are designed to introduce you to the basic rules found within the game:

Base Stats

Similar to games made with the Tri-Stat System, such as “Big Eyes, Small Mouth”, this system utilizes only three Base Stats; Physique, Ego, and Instinct.

Physique: This Base Stat represents your physical presence and being. The higher it is, the heartier and more physically threatening you are.

Ego: This Base Stat represents your mental strength and knowledge. The higher it is, the more inquisitive and mentally threatening you are.

Instinct: This Base Stat represents your willpower and subconscious nature. The higher it is, the more naturally intelligent and strong you are.

Aptitudes

Each character made with this system has a set of Aptitudes that tell you how effective they are with certain abilities or tasks. Each Aptitude has an assigned value from -50 to +50 which directly influences your d% Checks. If multiple Aptitudes are viable for a given d% Check, use the highest Aptitude value applicable to the situation.

d% Checks

This system’s Checks rely on rolls made with two 10-sided dice, or more commonly known as d%, to determine their overall outcome. Roll results made with d% range from 1 to 100. Rolling double zeroes results in the maximum of 100.

Checks have a Target Value and utilize the following formula for comparison: Stat + Aptitude + d%.

If the Target Value is less than or equal to the formula’s result or you roll 96 or higher, the Check is a Success. Otherwise, if the Target Value is less than or equal to the formula’s result or you roll 5 or lower, it’s a Failure. Additionally, if a Success result overcomes the Target Value by 50 or more, the Check is a Critical Success. On the other hand, if the Target Value overcomes a Failure result by 50 or more, it’s a Critical Failure. Critical Successes and Critical Failures change the severity of the result’s outcome, for better or worse.

Contested d% Checks

If two or more opposing characters within the game interact, the way to determine the outcome’s results are through Contested d% Checks. For the most part, Contested d% Checks are performed using the same rules as normal, except that the defender’s Target Value is also determined with Stat + Aptitude + d%. If the defender succeeds, the attacker fails, and vice versa.

Abilities and Action Points

This system utilizes a pool of Actions Points you can use on your turn to perform Abilities while in a dangerous situation, such as combat or hazardous areas. When not in a dangerous situation, Abilities that cost Action Points take 2 seconds per Action Point spent to perform them. Abilities can cost anywhere from 0 to 3 Action Points, among other requirements and features dictated by their Properties.

Effect Mitigation

Unlike other more noteworthy systems, this system has both Defense and Evasion as Effect Mitigation. Evasion functions as though it’s an Aptitude, meaning it scales from -50 to +50, and is your primary Effect Mitigation. Defense functions as a flat value with potential restrictions, with that value reducing incoming damage related to its restrictions to a minimum of 0, and is your secondary Effect Mitigation.

Core Philosophies

The Core Philosophies of this TTRPG system will help you understand what to expect from its design and intent. They are as followed:

  • Action Inspired: This will be mostly a combat-focused RPG, relying on action sequences inspired by comic books, fighting games, and action media tropes.
  • Simplicity First: The main functionality of the game should be simple to understand at first glance. With this in mind, abilities and the effects they can produce should be short and to the point.
  • Unleashed Potential: The game should feel impactful to play, allowing the players to reach heights of both power and creativity within your games.
  • Clear Expectations: Whether playing the game or hosting a session, people have a shared goal in mind; To have fun with the game, cooperating in terms of storytelling and gameplay. With great power comes even greater responsibility; The expectations you and your players have should align closely enough to not cause problems that last outside of the game.
